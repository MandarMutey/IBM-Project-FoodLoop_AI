Surplus goes to waste

Hostel messes, caterers and wedding halls have extra food but do not know who needs it right now.

How one rescue works

Times below are targets for a pilot, not measured results.

  1. Donor sends a message

    One WhatsApp text: what is left, how much, when it was cooked.

    Under 1 minute

  2. Listing is created

    The Donor agent reads the text and works out the safe-until time.

    Seconds

  3. Best receiver is offered

    The Matcher ranks NGOs and sends the top offer, with reasons.

    Offer in 2 minutes

  4. Pickup is booked

    Logistics picks the nearest volunteer and checks the delivery time.

    Confirmed in 10 minutes

  5. Proof and report

Five agents, one rescue desk

Donor

Reads the message and lists food, quantity and safe-until time.

Output: a listing with a countdown.

Matcher

Ranks NGOs by need, distance, capacity and time left.

Output: ranked receivers with reasons.

Logistics

Assigns the nearest volunteer and a pickup slot.

Output: pickup and delivery times.

Safety and Impact

Output: delivery receipt and report.

Coordinator agent keeps shared memory of donors, receivers and volunteers, and sends any unclear case to a human. It also writes the log you can see in the demo.

Questions people ask

Why a 4-hour safe window?

It is a planning assumption for this concept project. A real pilot must follow local food-safety rules (in India, FSSAI guidance) and use the stricter limit for each type of food.

What if the donor's message is unclear?

The Donor agent shows what it understood. If quantity or cooked time is missing, the Coordinator sends the case to a human instead of guessing. Try the "unclear message" sample in the demo.

What if a volunteer does not show up?

The next volunteer is asked after 10 minutes. If the pickup still fails, a human coordinator takes over before the safe window ends.

Does this demo send real messages?

No. Everything runs in your browser on sample NGOs, volunteers and numbers. Nothing is sent and no personal data is stored.

Who pays for it?

NGOs never pay. Revenue comes from CSR sponsorship per meal, chain and caterer subscriptions, ESG reports, and college and municipal contracts.

Pilot design

Run for one semester with hostel messes and two NGOs in one Tier-2 city. Compare meals reaching NGOs against the previous semester. Publish the results.

  • Weeks 1 to 2: onboard two NGOs, three messes, five volunteers
  • Weeks 3 to 6: Donor and Matcher agents live, calls as backup
  • Weeks 7 to 14: add Logistics and Safety agents
  • Weeks 15 to 18: collect results and publish

How we will know it worked

  • More meals reach NGOs than in the previous semester
  • Most food is delivered with more than 30 minutes of safe time left
  • Donors list again without being reminded
  • No food-safety incident in the whole pilot

If these are not met, we publish that too and change the design.

What is real in this demo

  • Message reading, using simple pattern rules
  • Ranking with the formula shown on screen
  • Safe-time check and expired-food block
  • Volunteer choice and delivery times
  • Meal counting and the downloadable receipt

What is simulated

  • NGO acceptance and volunteer replies
  • WhatsApp sending: no messages are sent
  • Photo proof and signature
  • NGOs, volunteers and areas are sample data

In the planned product, a language model would read the message and return the same fields.

Safety and privacy

Cooked food with a time stamp only. Hygiene checklist and delivery proof. Collect only what is needed, with consent. Every match shows its reasons.

Human in the loop

A person is asked when the message is unclear, the safe window has passed, no volunteer confirms, or a safety incident is reported. Agents never guess on food safety.
